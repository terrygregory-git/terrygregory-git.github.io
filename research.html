Working Papers

Mapping Artificial Intelligence: Evidence from Firm-Level Web Data in Europe R&R Research Policy
with Julio Garbers
IZA Discussion Paper No. 18434, 2026
IZA DP 18434 Data
Abstract

We develop a firm-level AI adoption indicator for Europe by applying large language models to more than three million firm websites between 2016 and 2024. The share of AI-active firms grew from 1% to 12%, with the ecosystem shifting from specialized AI providers to broader adopters. Foundational data skills form the necessary base for adoption, while specialized AI skills act as strong complements.

Firm-level Technology Adoption in Times of Crisis R&R European Economic Review
with Melanie Arntz, Michael Böhm, Georg Graetz, Florian Lehmer, and Cäcilia Lipowski
ZEW Discussion Paper No. 24-057, 2024
ZEW DP 24-057 Policy Brief
Abstract

We examine technology investments before and during the Covid-19 pandemic in German firms. Investment in frontier technologies declined sharply, equivalent to losing 1.4 years of pre-pandemic investment activity. Post-pandemic investments increasingly facilitated remote work capabilities.

Minimum Wages and the Rise in Solo Self-Employment R&R ILR Review
with Ulrich Zierahn-Weilage and Angelika Ganserer
IZA Discussion Paper No. 15283
IZA DP 15283
Abstract

We document that the adoption of industry-specific minimum wages doubled solo self-employment shares in high-impact areas, suggesting that workers were involuntarily pushed into more precarious alternative work arrangements with poorer social security.

Impact of Firm-Level Technology Adoption on Workers under revision
with Sabrina Genz and Florian Lehmer
IZA Discussion Paper No. 14626
IZA DP 14626
Abstract

Using linked employer-employee data, we study how firm-level technology adoption affects worker outcomes. Technology adoption increases employment stability and wages; effects vary by occupation and adoption strategy, with augmenting technologies generating sizable gains compared with limited benefits from substitutive technologies.

Evolving Skill Demands in the Age of AI: Evidence from Europe under revision
with Najada Feimi, Christina Gathmann, and David Marguerit
2026
Abstract

Analyzing 31 million online job ads from four European countries, we document significant growth in the demand for AI, data, and prediction skills in exposed occupations, but a decline in social skills since 2018, alongside greater skill specialization.
